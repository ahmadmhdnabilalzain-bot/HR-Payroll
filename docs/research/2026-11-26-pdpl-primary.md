## Executive regulations: status

- Art 28 (READ): "The Council of Ministers, based upon a suggestion from
  the General Director of the Bureau, shall issue the Executive
  Regulations of this Decree by Law within six (6) months as of the date
  on which the Decree by Law is promulgated." (Deadline ~Mar 2022.)
- Art 29 (READ): controllers and processors "shall regularize their
  status ... within a period of no more than six (6) months as of the
  date on which its Executive Regulations are issued. The Council of
  Ministers may extend such period for another similar period."
- Evidence of issuance: NOT FOUND in primary sources. The official
  uaelegislation.gov.ae page lists no executive regulation or Cabinet
  decision tied to the law.
- SUMMARY ONLY (secondary, unverified against a primary source):
  - priviq.com: "As of October 2026 the executive regulations and the
    Cabinet penalty decision have not been issued".
  - iconnectitbs.com: regulations not issued as of mid-2026; no
    adequacy list or standard contractual clauses published.
  - business-ciso.com and others: Cabinet created the Federal Authority
    for Artificial Intelligence and Data on 14 June 2026, absorbing the
    Emirates Data Office; FTI Consulting (17 Nov 2026) describes the
    same merger of the AI Office, a TDRA sector and the Data Office.
- Conclusion: the law is in force since 2 Jan 2022 (READ). The
  executive regulations appear NOT issued as of Nov 2026 (secondary
  only). The six-month regularisation clock in Art 29 therefore has
  not started on this reading. Re-check before launch.
- Note: the text says "Bureau"/"Office" for the UAE Data Office (Art 1).

## Scope and exemptions (Arts 2, 3)

- Art 2(1) (READ): applies to processing "by means of electronic systems
  which operate automatically, or by other means" of data of (a) a Data
  Subject "residing in the State or having a place of business in it";
  (b) a controller/processor in the State, for data subjects inside or
  outside; (c) a controller/processor outside the State processing data
  of data subjects inside the State.
- Art 2(2) (READ) does NOT apply to: "a. Government Data; b. Governmental
  entities which control or process Personal Data; c. Personal Data held
  by the security and judicial authorities; d. A Data Subject who
  processes his/her data for personal purposes; e. Personal Health Data
  that has legislation regulating its protection and processing;
  f. Personal banking and credit data and information that have
  legislation regulating their protection and processing; g. Companies
  and establishments located in free zones in the Country and have
  special legislations regarding Personal Data protection."
- Employment: NOT FOUND as an exemption. No employee-data carve-out
  exists; employment appears only as a lawful basis (Art 4(8)).
- Free zones: 2(2)(g) exempts free-zone entities only where the free
  zone has its own data protection legislation. DIFC and ADGM are
  NOT named in the federal text (NOT FOUND); that they have their own
  laws is SUMMARY ONLY (business-ciso.com snippet). Other free zones
  without their own law are not exempt on the text.
- Art 3 (READ): the Bureau "may exempt some establishments that do not
  process a large volume of Personal Data" from some or all
  requirements, per standards in the Executive Regulations (pending).
- Banking/credit: 2(2)(f) matters for the payroll bank-data fields.
  Whether employer-held IBAN data counts as "banking data" with its
  own legislation is NOT resolved by the text.

## Definitions (Art 1, READ)

- Personal Data: "Any data related to a specific natural person or
  related to a natural person that can be identified directly or
  indirectly ... name, voice, image, identification number, his/her
  electronic identifier, his/her geographical location, or by one or
  more physical, physiological, economic, cultural or social
  characteristics. It includes Sensitive Personal Data and Biometric
  Data". So location data and voice are named identifiers.
- Sensitive Personal Data: "directly or indirectly reveals a natural
  person's family, ethnic origin, political or philosophical opinions,
  religious beliefs, criminal record, biometric data, or any data
  relating to such person's health and physical, psychological, mental,
  genetic or sexual condition". Salary and bank data are not listed.
- Biometric Data: personal data from processing "related to the
  physical, physiological or behavioral characteristics ... which
  allows the identification or confirmation of the unique
  identification of the Data Subject, such as facial images or
  fingerprints". (Relevant to face/fingerprint attendance.)
- Profiling: automated processing "to assess certain personality
  aspects ... including ... financial performance or condition, health,
  personal preferences, interests, behavior, location, movements or
  reliability". (Relevant to WFH tracking and scoring.)
- Consent: authorisation that "indicates, in a specific, clear and
  unambiguous manner ... through a clear positive statement or action".
- Also defined: Pseudonymisation, Anonymization, Data Breach,
  Cross-Border Processing, Establishment, Controller, Processor, DPO.

## Lawful bases (Arts 4, 5)

- Art 4 (READ): "It is prohibited to process Personal Data without the
  consent of its owner." Exceptions include: (1) public interest;
  (2) data made public by the data subject; (3) legal claims or
  judicial/security procedures; (4) occupational or preventive medicine
  "to assess the employees' ability to work"; (7) protect the data
  subject's interests; (8) "necessary for the purposes of the
  Controller or Data Subject carrying out their obligations and
  exercising their legally established rights in the field of
  employment, social security or laws concerned with social
  protection, to the extent permitted by such Laws"; (9) "necessary to
  perform a contract to which the Data Subject is a party"; (10)
  "necessary to fulfil specific obligations stipulated in other laws";
  (11) other cases in the Executive Regulations.
- There is NO general "legitimate interests" basis in the text (NOT
  FOUND). Payroll and statutory records map to 4(8)-(10). Tracking,
  meeting recording and analytics do not map cleanly and may need
  consent or another basis.
- Art 5 principles (READ): fair, transparent, lawful; specific and
  clear purpose, no incompatible later use; "sufficient and limited to
  what is necessary"; accurate and updated; secure; and (5(7)) "shall
  not be kept after the purpose of its processing has been exhausted.
  It may be kept if ... concealed using the 'Anonymization
  Mechanism'".

## Consent and employee consent (Art 6)

- Art 6(1) (READ): controller must prove consent; consent must be
  "clear, simple, unambiguous and easily accessible ... in writing or
  electronically"; it "shall include the Data Subject's right to
  withdraw it easily".
- Art 6(2): "may, at any time, withdraw their consent ... shall not
  affect the legality of the processing based on the given consent
  before withdrawing it."
- Employee-specific consent rule, power-imbalance or "freely given"
  test: NOT FOUND. "Freely given" does not appear in the text.
- Withdrawal links to erasure: Art 15(2)(b) (READ).

## Controller duties (Art 7, READ)

- Technical and organisational measures for confidentiality and
  integrity; measures "while determining the means of processing"
  incl. Pseudonymisation; automatic settings limiting "volume and type
  of Personal Data collected ... the period of storage and
  accessibility" (privacy by default).
- 7(4): keep "a special record for Personal Data" with controller and
  DPO data, categories of data, persons authorised to access,
  "processing times, limitations and scope", erase/modify mechanism,
  purpose, "any data related to the cross-border movement", and
  security measures; submit to the Bureau on request.
- 7(5): appoint a processor "which has sufficient guarantees".

## Processor duties (Art 8, READ)

- Process per controller instructions and a contract defining scope,
  subject, purpose, nature, data type, data subject category.
- Notify controller if processing exceeds the agreed period; "Erase
  data after the expiry of the processing period or upon handing it
  over to the Controller" (8(4)); keep its own record (8(7));
  written contract between multiple processors, else joint liability
  (8(10)). Details deferred to Executive Regulations (8(11)).
- Art 20 (READ) security: "Encryption of Personal Data and the
  application of Pseudonymisation", resilience, timely restore, and
  regular testing, risk-based, "best international practices".

## Breach notification (Art 9, READ)

- Controller notifies the Bureau on becoming aware of a breach that
  "would prejudice the privacy, confidentiality and security of data",
  "within the period and in accordance with the measures ... set by the
  Executive Regulations". Deadline in hours/days: NOT FOUND (left to
  the unissued regulations).
- Report content (9(1)): nature, form, causes, approximate number of
  records; DPO details; likely effects; corrective measures; documents;
  other Bureau requirements.
- 9(2): controller also notifies the Data Subject if privacy is
  prejudiced, same deferral. 9(3): "If the Processor becomes aware of
  any breach ... it shall notify the Controller ... as soon as it
  becomes aware", and the controller then informs the Bureau.

## Data Protection Officer (Arts 10-12, READ)

- Art 10(1): controller and processor "shall appoint" a DPO "in any of
  the following cases": (a) high risk from new technologies or data
  volume; (b) "systematic and comprehensive assessment of Sensitive
  Personal Data, including Profiling and Automated Processing";
  (c) "a large volume of Sensitive Personal Data".
- 10(2): DPO may be an employee or authorised person, "inside or
  outside the State". 10(3): publish contact details and notify the
  Bureau. 10(4): technologies and volume thresholds are left to the
  Executive Regulations (NOT FOUND).
- Art 11 tasks: verify procedures, receive requests and complaints,
  technical advice and periodic evaluation, liaison with the Bureau,
  confidentiality. Art 12: involve DPO timely, provide resources, "Not
  to terminate the Data Protection Officer services or impose any
  disciplinary penalty for a reason related to the performance of its
  duties"; data subjects may contact the DPO directly.

## Impact assessment (Art 21, READ)

- Before processing, assess impact "when using any of the modern
  technologies that would pose a high risk". Required (21(2)) for
  (a) "systematic and comprehensive assessment of the personal aspects
  of the Data Subject, using automated processing, including
  profiling, having legal consequences or serious impact", and
  (b) "a large volume of Sensitive Personal Data".
- Minimum content (21(3)): description and purpose; necessity and
  proportionality; risks; mitigation. One assessment may cover similar
  operations (21(4)); coordinate with the DPO (21(5)); the Bureau will
  publish a list of exempt operation types (21(6), NOT FOUND yet);
  review regularly (21(7)).

## Data subject rights (Arts 13-19, 24, 25, READ)

- Art 13 access, "without any consideration" (free): types of data,
  purposes, automated decisions, recipients inside/outside the State,
  storage period criteria, correction/erasure procedures,
  cross-border safeguards, breach actions, how to complain. 13(2):
  before processing provide purposes, recipients and cross-border
  safeguards. 13(3): refusal allowed if repetitive, conflicts with
  investigations, harms security, or affects third parties.
- Art 14 portability: machine-readable copy where processing is by
  consent or contract and "carried out by automated means"; transfer
  to another controller "whenever it is technically feasible".
- Art 15 correction and erasure (grounds: no longer necessary;
  consent withdrawn; objection; unlawful processing). 15(3) no erasure
  where it affects claims/defence of rights, or conflicts with other
  legislation the controller is subject to. Retention-law conflicts
  (e.g. labour or tax record rules) fall here.
- Art 16 restriction; 16(2): data subject may ask the controller to
  keep data after purpose ends for claims. Art 17 objection (direct
  marketing, statistical surveys, violations of Art 5).
- Art 18: right to object to automated decisions incl. profiling with
  "legal impact"; not available where agreed in the contract, required
  by law, or prior consent given; 18(4): "The Controller shall include
  the human element in reviewing automated processing decisions at the
  request of the Data Subject."
- Art 19: clear ways to contact the controller. Art 24: complaints to
  the Bureau. Art 25: grievance within 30 days, decided within 30 days.
- Response deadline for requests (days): NOT FOUND.

## Cross-border transfer (Arts 22, 23, READ)

- Art 22: transfer "in the following cases approved by the Bureau":
  destination has data protection legislation covering key provisions,
  rights and "a judicial or regulatory authority imposing appropriate
  measures"; or the State is party to a relevant bilateral or
  multilateral agreement. No adequacy list found (NOT FOUND).
- Art 23(1): where protection is not available, transfer allowed:
  (a) under a contract obligating the foreign party to adopt this
  law's measures; (b) "explicit consent ... to transfer his/her
  Personal Data outside the State, provided that such transfer shall
  not contradict the public or security interest of the State";
  (c) legal claims; (d) "necessary to sign or implement a contract
  made between the Controller and the Data Subject, or ... third
  parties to serve the interest of the Data Subject"; (e) international
  judicial cooperation; (f) public interest. 23(2): controls are left
  to the Executive Regulations (NOT FOUND).
- Data localisation (mandatory in-country storage) is NOT FOUND in
  this law. Sector rules (health, banking, government) are separate and
  were not researched here.

## Retention

- No fixed period in the law (NOT FOUND). Only Art 5(7) (purpose
  exhaustion, anonymisation exception), Art 8(4) (processor erasure),
  Art 13(1)(e) (disclose storage criteria), Art 15(3)(c) and 16(2)
  (keep for claims or other laws). Labour/tax retention periods come
  from other statutes (see 2026-11-25-labour-law-rules.md).

## Penalties and enforcement (Arts 24-27, READ)

- Art 26 (READ), in full effect: "The Council of Ministers, based upon
  a suggestion from the General Director of the Bureau, shall issue a
  decision to limit the actions which constitute a violation of this
  Decree by Law and its Executive Regulations, including
  administrative penalties to be imposed."
- No fine amounts, no criminal penalties, no compensation clause in
  the text (NOT FOUND). Cabinet penalty decision: NOT FOUND (secondary
  sources say not issued).
- Art 9(4): the Bureau verifies breaches and imposes Art 26 penalties.
  Art 27: Cabinet may delegate some Bureau powers to local authorities.

## Implications for the product (design suggestions only)

- Treat the law as in force with unissued details: build to the stated
  articles and keep thresholds, deadlines and fees configurable.
- Data inventory per field (Art 7(4) record): purpose, basis, access
  roles, retention rule, cross-border flag. Export on request.
- Tag each processing purpose with its Art 4 basis. Payroll, contract
  and statutory records: 4(8)-(10). Meeting recordings, transcripts,
  location tracking and productivity scoring: no clear basis, so gate
  them behind explicit, logged, per-purpose consent with easy
  withdrawal (Art 6), and an off-by-default setting.
- Location and voice are named identifiers (Art 1). Collect only the
  minimum for WFH (for example check-in zone, not continuous GPS);
  show a notice before processing (Art 13(2)).
- Face or fingerprint attendance is biometric and sensitive; avoid or
  isolate it. Sensitive data at scale triggers DPO and impact
  assessment duties (Arts 10, 21); ship a DPIA template.
- Salary and bank data are not listed as sensitive, but encrypt and
  pseudonymise anyway (Art 20); restrict by role; log access.
- Automated scoring or flags (profiling) need a human review path on
  request (Art 18(4)) and an explanation view.
- Build data subject tooling: access, correction, export in
  machine-readable form, restriction, erasure with legal-hold override
  (Arts 13-17); a DPO contact field and request log.
- Retention engine: delete or anonymise when purpose ends (Art 5(7)),
  with legal-hold flags for labour and tax duties.
- Breach workflow: incident log capturing the Art 9(1) fields, with
  processor-to-controller alerts and a configurable notification clock.
- Cross-border: record storage region per tenant; default to UAE
  region; for any foreign sub-processor, store the Art 23 basis
  (contract clause or explicit consent) and keep the list visible.
- Free-zone customers (DIFC, ADGM): separate regimes; flag tenant
  jurisdiction and confirm with counsel.
- Have counsel confirm the Arabic text and current status before
  launch.
