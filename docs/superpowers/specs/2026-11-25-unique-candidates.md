Asked by the owner on 2026-11-25. **Nothing here is in the plan yet.** The
owner picks. Every item is a bet until a demo proves it. "Not found" means

## Honest starting point

Any single feature can be copied. The lasting edge is the **engine these
features sit on**: a versioned, source-cited rule library, a per-line trace,
and an accuracy track record. The candidates below follow from that engine.

## Candidates, hardest to copy first

1. **Rule-change preview.** Before a law or rule change takes effect, show
   each company which payslips change and by how much. Needs the versioned
   rule library. Rival pages only say "updates automatically". [BET]
2. **Verifiable payslips and salary certificates.** A code that a bank or
   landlord checks online. Bayzat's page says employees use payslips for
   banks, visas and loans. [BET]
3. **What-if runs on live data.** The full cost of a hire, or the settlement
   if someone leaves on a given date, computed by the real engine with the
   trace. Standalone calculators exist; the live-data version is the edge.
   [BET]
4. **Pay explained to the employee** in their own language, line by line
   from the trace. Keka claims local-language access; its depth is unknown.
   [BET]
5. **Fine exposure in dirhams** across WPS dates, Emiratisation and expiring
   documents. Needs sourced penalty amounts, which we do not have yet. [BET]
6. **Keep-your-spreadsheet mode and switch importers.** Import a monthly
   Excel, validate, produce the file, with no migration; read Zoho and Bayzat
   exports to produce the parallel-run report. Aims at the spreadsheet users
   the market research found. [BET]

Keep out: earned-wage access and salary cards (payments, licensing).

## Two leads on rival correctness (check in a trial; do not assert)

- **Bayzat** mentions "limited and unlimited contracts" on its HR page. The
  official labour law says contracts are for a definite period (Article 8,
  clause 3) and that indefinite contracts had to be converted within one
  year of the law taking effect (Article 68, clause 2). The wording may just
  be outdated; it does not prove a wrong calculation.
- **Keka's** help article on gratuity gives a UAE example of AED 24,657.53
  for AED 10,000 monthly salary and five years (annual salary over 365, times
  15 days, times 5). MOHRE's stated rate is 21 days per year for the first
  five years, which gives about AED 35,000 for the same case. The article may
  be a generic example, not the product's UAE logic. The same article also
  says the law does not prescribe how to compute the daily wage (30-day or
  365-day), so our engine exposes the divisor as an explicit, sourced setting.
